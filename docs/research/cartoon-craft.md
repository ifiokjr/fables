# What makes a really good cartoon, and what fables is missing

Research for fables, 18 November 2026. Renders looked at (not kept):
*The Visitor* proofs, storyboard (two sheets), review and landscape preview video (108 s, tiled at 1 fps
and at 6 fps around the climax and the entrance); *Stinky McStinkbreath* proofs and review; one proof
page each from the mid-century, clear-line, manga and anime test worlds. Sources are cited inline.
Practitioner blogs are marked *(practitioner)*; research is cited by its paper.

## 1. Ten things that matter most

1. **The picture shows what the words say happened.** Preschoolers spend over 90% of shared reading
   looking at the pictures, not the print, so an action that is only narrated is an action they never
   see ([Evans & Saint-Aubin 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2005.01636.x)).
2. **One goal, stated early, with every event caused by the one before.** Children recall events with
   more causal links better, and goal structure helps preschoolers' recall
   ([van den Broek, Lorch & Thurlow 1996](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/14140375_Children's_and_Adults'_Memory_for_Television_Stories_The_Role_of_Causal_Factors_Story-Grammar_Categories_and_Hierarchical_Level);
   [goal structure study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8459508_Preschoolers'_comprehension_of_goal_structure_in_narratives)).
3. **Repetition with variation: threes, refrains and chains.** Repeat viewing raised preschoolers'
   comprehension and joining in ([Crawley et al. 1999](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232578269_Effects_of_Repeated_Exposures_to_a_Single_Episode_of_the_Television_Program_Blue's_Clues_on_the_Viewing_Behaviors_and_Comprehension_of_Preschool_Children)),
   and folk tales run on the law of three and repetition ([Olrik's epic laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_Laws_of_Folk_Narrative)).
4. **Calm and grounded beats fast and frantic.** Nine minutes of a fast, fantastical cartoon lowered
   four-year-olds' executive function ([Lillard & Peterson 2011](https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/128/4/644/30711/The-Immediate-Impact-of-Different-Types-of)),
   and impossible events mattered more than pace ([Lillard et al. 2015](https://www.ovid.com/journals/depsy/abstract/10.1037/a0039097~further-examination-of-the-immediate-impact-of-television-on)).
   Slow narration and time to look are features.
5. **Acting is thought you can see.** A character notices, thinks, acts, then reacts: eyes first, then
   head, then body ([the "thinking character"](https://pozervision.wordpress.com/2025/01/07/john-lasseters-animation-tricks-and-trade-secrets-from-pixar/)),
   with time to process a change of feeling ([emotional hang time](https://blog.animschool.edu/2024/03/14/animators-are-actors/)).
   The reaction always comes after its cause.
6. **Two to a scene, built as opposites, read by silhouette.** Olrik's laws of two to a scene and of
   contrast ([epic laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_Laws_of_Folk_Narrative)); one dominant shape
   family per character and separated silhouettes ([shape language](https://coartist.net/blog/character-design-readability-silhouette-shape-language-contrast), *practitioner*).
7. **Direction on the page means something.** Rightward is setting out and leftward is coming home
   ([page composition](http://thewendywatsonblog.blogspot.com/2012/05/picture-book-design-page-composition.html), *practitioner*;
   [returning home](http://mattobin.blogspot.com/2020/06/exploring-pictures-in-picturebooks.html)).
   Within a scene, keep to one side of the line ([continuity editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuity_editing)).
8. **The page turn is the suspense engine.** Set up before the turn, reveal after it, and give the
   reveal time to be looked at ([page turns](https://www.darcypattison.com/writing/picture-books/picture-book-standards-32-pages/);
   [dummy exercise](https://www.novlr.org/the-reading-room/so-youve-written-a-picture-book-what-next)).
9. **Sound steers attention.** Sound effects and distinctive voices pull three-to-five-year-olds back to
   the screen and help them understand, while abstract adult narration does not
   ([Calvert et al. 1982](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-Relation-Between-Selective-Attention-to-Forms-Calvert-Huston/38afa92cd571e0203caa68e608d724d05ccfcc3a);
   [formal features](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227590435_Communicating_More_than_Content_Formal_Features_of_Children's_Television_Programs)).
   Music should change with the story, and silence is a cue in its own right.
10. **A style is a set of rules it never breaks, and timing is judged in motion before final.** Lock
    line, shade, palette, proportion, light and finish ([style bible](https://studioliddell.com/news/animation-art-direction-a-practical-guide-for-2026), *practitioner*).
    The animatic is where pacing problems show up ([traditional animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_animation)).

## 2. Gap analysis

### Story

- **Well.** `docs/directing.md` is good craft: want, obstacle and change; one idea a page; the rule
  of three; the refrain; read-aloud word budgets by age; the page turn as a reveal. The Kerfuffle
  recipe and *Stinky* carry it out. The habit shows on the outside (the stink cloud, wilting flowers,
  fainting birds and postman), three consequences are reversed in the callback pages, NOPE gets an
  "ALREADY DONE!" button, and a hidden worm on every page is a participation game (proof contact sheet).
- **Badly.** *The Visitor* fits two story events into one page: proof page 5 (`08-story-05.png`) has
  the string lost *and* Mo catching it. `directing.md` gives an "and then" test for this, but no review
  rule applies it. The review reads the script and never the picture, and for *The Visitor* it says
  "Nothing a director would change" although its kite is never drawn (see Visual). There is one recipe,
  and it is a habit tale.
- **Not at all.** No other story flows. Nothing checks for a refrain, a goal stated early, a return
  image or a wind-down. No participation beat in the video. Nothing plans page counts: *The Visitor*
  has 8 story pages, then an empty "Meet the folk" blob (proof 12) and twelve blank pages (proofs
  14–25) to reach KDP's 24.

### Visual storytelling

- **Well.** The colour script works. *The Visitor* moves through dawn, day, golden, storm, dusk and
  moonlit, and the low point is the darkest page (contact sheet, pages 1–8), which the colour-script
  idea calls for ([colour scripts](https://ideas.ted.com/how-color-helps-a-movie-tell-its-story/)).
  The readme's head-framing claim holds in every panel I checked; storyboard panel
  "page 3 · close on Hana" has clean headroom. Stills frame clear of the text card. Closes get depth of field.
- **Badly.** Text and picture do not agree. In *The Visitor* no kite or string is drawn on any page,
  though the story is about a kite. Proof page 3 says Mo "hid his mouth behind his hands", but Mo is
  not in the frame. On page 4 Hana "held out the string" with her hands on her hips. Page 8 says
  "coiled her string" and "Goodbye", but there is no string and no wave. The root cause is that `holds=`
  knows three hard-coded props (`src/stage.rs:639`). Otto's ruler on the mid-century page is missing
  for the same reason.
  - Compositions repeat. In *Stinky*, the star stands about a third of the way in, at full-shot size,
    facing right, on most pages. Every story page in both books puts the card at the top, and in
    *The Visitor* it covers 35–40% of the page.
  - Pages 6 and 7 of *The Visitor* (garden, dusk, same positions) look identical. `directing.md` says
    the review catches this, but it does not: `same_stage` counts `grin` versus `happy` as a change
    (`src/review.rs:136`).
  - On page 5, Mo (x=0.5) and Hana (x=0.7) overlap in silhouette, with Mo's head over her arm. This
    passes `crowding`, because that rule measures x distance and not the drawn shapes.
- **Not at all.** No screen-direction or 180° continuity. No insert or cut-in on an object (there are
  no objects). No eyeline to anything but a speaker. No two-page spreads, which is a known limit and
  fine (see section 5).

### Performance

- **Well.** Hana's entrance at video 0:22–0:24 is a real walk: planted feet, arm swing, a turn on
  arrival. Listeners look at the speaker. The ten acting rules exist, have dials, and differ by style
  (`docs/animation.md`). Takes, breathing and idle business are all present.
- **Badly.**
  - Takes freeze. At video 0:16–0:28 Mo holds the open-mouthed `shocked` face for about 12 s. The
    "settle" of rule 5 settles back into the same face, so the take never finishes.
  - Reactions come before their causes. On page 2, `@mo shocked` is written before Hana enters, so the
    take plays at 0:16 and she walks on at 0:22.
  - The climax is a non-event. At 0:59–1:03, while the narrator says "ran, and jumped, and caught the
    string", Mo steps 0.15 of the stage and holds a smug eyes-closed `proud` grin with his arms down
    (6 fps sheet).
  - Hana has both hands on her hips on 6 of the 7 pages she is on.
  - Everyone of one style moves alike: the motion dials live in the style, and the cast has no motion
    of its own.
- **Not at all.** No thought before action (notice, beat, decide). No look at an object or off screen.
  No contact with objects (give, catch, pull). No double take, and no dead-stop hold for comedy
  ([comic timing](https://digicel.net/why-does-animation-timing-matter/), *practitioner*). Speakers in
  dialogue never turn towards each other: they stand three-quarter to camera with only their eyes
  sideways (proof page 8).

### Sound

- **Well.** Cast voices with mood-driven delivery. Shouted words are shouted. Nine sound effects, with
  automatic bonk, boing and pop. The music ducks under speech (`src/video/audio.rs:345`). The mix is
  mastered to −16 LUFS.
- **Badly.** There is one four-bar I–vi–IV–V ukulele loop at 104 bpm under the whole film
  (`src/video/audio.rs:221`): the storm page, "Oh no… gone", and the moonlit goodbye all get the same
  bouncy strum.
  - Narration is fast for three-to-fives. The default narrator speaks at 165 wpm (`src/video/voice.rs:79`),
    and *The Visitor* averages about 155 wpm including every gap (256 words in about 98 s between the
    title and end cards). Practitioner guidance for picture
    books is about 120–140 wpm ([pace](https://www.endoral.com/blog/reading-aloud-vs-reading-silently-pace/),
    *practitioner*), against about 155 wpm for adult audiobooks.
  - Captions merge speakers: cue 19 holds Hana's line and the narrator's "Mo stood tall" for 9 s.
- **Not at all.** No silence before a peak. No stingers. No ambience (wind in a book about wind; rain
  on the storm page). No music change at the story's turns. No sound on an action's contact frame
  (sound effects land 2–5 frames after an impact, [SFX timing](https://sfxengine.com/blog/sound-effects-timing-in-comedy-videos), *practitioner*).

### Process

- **Well.** The process is the studio pipeline: script, then review, storyboard, frames, preview and
  final. The storyboard is fast, labels shot size, subject and duration, and flags `· short` holds. The
  MCP loop lets an agent see what it made. The contact sheet doubles as a colour script.
- **Badly.**
  - The storyboard is a still sheet, and the timed check is the 70-second preview video. An animatic
    is the cheap place to find pacing problems ([what an animatic catches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_animation)),
    and fables makes it the expensive one.
  - Slow pushes look like duplicate panels: "page 4 · wide · 6.8s" then "page 4 · wide · 1.2s". Every
    panel repeats the page's "wipe in".
  - The review's advice contradicts the director. `directing.md` says `@pause 0.8` lets a reaction
    land, but the director's floor is 1.2 s (`src/video/director.rs:49`), so Hana's shocked reaction on
    page 5 never gets the close that the readme promises: no such panel exists.
- **Not at all.** No thumbnail or dummy pass that plans pages against the print count. No check that
  compares the words with the picture.

### Style range

- **Well.** Ten drawing styles as data, with real rules: mid-century snaps to an eight-colour palette
  and holds on twos, manga screens every colour to ink and paper, and clear-line uses one line weight.
  Guests keep their own style, which turns style contrast into story (anime Hana beside cartoon Mo).
  The four test pages each read as one look.
- **Badly.** Coherence stops at the character. The text card is the same cream rounded card in the same
  face in every style (Otto, Zia, Ren and Hana pages side by side). The best skin for each style is
  advice in the readme (riso for mid-century, clear for clear-line) rather than data, so a world can
  pair them wrongly.
- **Not at all.** Every built-in drawing style is outlined, so there is no line-free look, though
  `[line] stroke = "none"` exists. There is no silhouette or shadow-theatre style, which fits a cut-out
  rig naturally, and no paper-theatre style.

## 3. Proposals

Ranked by improvement for the effort. ★ marks the five to do first.

**★1. `text-picture` review rule (S).**
- *Viewer:* books stop narrating things nobody sees.
- *Mechanism:* a small lexicon in `review.rs` maps action verbs to the directions that show them
  (jumped→`jump`/`hop`; waved/goodbye→`wave`; ran→`run` or an `x` change; hid→`cover-mouth`/`hide`;
  pointed→`point`; sat→`sit`; laughed→`laugh`; cried→`sad`; held/gave/caught→`holds=`). Nouns are
  matched against prop names. A page whose text names a verb or prop that its directions and setting
  never show gets a `consider`.
- *Check:* *The Visitor* gets findings on pages 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8, and *Stinky* stays near clean.

**★2. Props as files (M).**
- *Viewer:* kites, rulers, spoons and books are drawn in hands.
- *Mechanism:* `props/<name>.svg` in a style or world, read the way settings are, with `data-grip="x y"`
  and `data-hands="one|two"`. `holds=<name>` resolves any file. The three built-ins move into files and
  the enum (`figure.rs:235`, `stage.rs:639`) goes. Add props to `style sheet` and MCP `prop_*` tools.
- *Check:* `holds=kite-spool` draws on proof page 4, and `fables style check` passes.

**★3. Eyelines: `see=` and `notice` (S).**
- *Viewer:* characters look up at the kite, at what is pointed at, and off stage, and think before
  they act.
- *Mechanism:* the actor word `see=<actor|prop|up|down|left|right|front>` aims the eyes, then the head,
  through the existing `lead` rule. `notice` plays rule 1 and then holds the look for about 0.5 s
  before the next direction lands. Listeners also follow a `point` to its target.
- *Check:* the acting-test video gets a notice beat. In *The Visitor* page 5 video, both heads tilt
  up as the kite goes.

**★4. Age band (S).**
- *Viewer and listener:* slower, clearer reading for the youngest.
- *Mechanism:* `age = "under-3" | "3-5" | "5-7"` in the front matter, with a world default. It sets
  the narrator's rate when none is given (about 125, 135 and 150 wpm), stretches `GAP` and `TAIL`
  (`timeline.rs:35-39`), and gives `word-budget` the age table that `directing.md` already prints.
- *Check:* `fables review` uses the band's limits, and the preview at `3-5` runs about 20% longer.

**★5. Music that follows the story (M).**
- *Listener:* the storm sounds tense, the goodbye sounds quiet, and the peak lands in silence.
- *Mechanism:* the generator in `audio.rs` makes cue variants from mode, tempo and voicing: `play`
  (today's), `calm`, `tense` (minor, pulse) and `sleepy` (music box, about 70 bpm). Each page's
  `light=` picks a default (storm→tense, dusk→calm, moonlit and candle→sleepy). Script words:
  `@music <cue>|stop` and `@sfx sting`. The bed drops out 0.5 s before a `**SHOUT**` on a strong mood
  and comes back after it.
- *Check:* an RMS plot of `soundtrack.wav` shows the change at the storm page and silence before
  "I HAVE IT!". Then listen to it.

**6. Reaction shots survive the floor (S).**
- *Viewer:* the shocked listener actually gets their close-up.
- *Mechanism:* when a Reaction event falls near the end of a page, the page's tail stretches to
  `MIN_SHOT` instead of `place_cut` dropping the reaction. `reactions` in the review suggests
  `@pause 1.2`, not `0.8`.
- *Check:* the page 5 storyboard gains "close on Hana · 1.2s".

**7. Takes that finish (M).**
- *Viewer:* a shock hits, holds, then relaxes into a living face.
- *Mechanism:* acting rule 11, dial `hang`. `shocked`, `disgusted` and `laugh` hold their peak for
  0.8–1.2 s, then ease to a quieter held version of the face (mouth closes, brows drop a notch). The
  book still keeps the full mood.
- *Check:* at video 0:17, Mo's mouth has closed.

**8. Loose and tethered props, with give and catch (M).**
- *Viewer:* a kite flies on a string, and Mo leaps and catches it.
- *Mechanism:* a prop as an actor, `@kite x=0.7 y=sky tether=hana`, drawn as a line from the holder's
  grip that sways like `data-drift`. `give=mo` brings both hands together and moves the prop across.
  `catch` is a reach-up pose that snaps the prop to the hand on the key. Builds on 2.
- *Check:* the 6 fps sheet of page 5 shows the leap, the reach and the string in Mo's hand.

**9. "Same page" means the same picture (S).**
- *Viewer:* no more back-to-back identical pages.
- *Mechanism:* `same_stage` compares setting, light, shot, and each figure's x (within 0.05), pose and
  stance, and ignores the mood enum, since moods are barely visible at proof size.
- *Check:* *The Visitor* pages 6 and 7 get a `static-page` finding.

**10. Narration frames its subject (S).**
- *Viewer:* "Mo stood tall" shows Mo.
- *Mechanism:* the auto director splits a paragraph at quote boundaries, and a narrated sentence that
  begins with an on-stage cast member's name or `born` name frames that actor. Captions split at the
  same boundaries.
- *Check:* the page 7 storyboard has a panel on Mo, and cue 19 of the `.srt` becomes two cues.

**11. Room to look (S).**
- *Viewer:* a new place is seen before it is talked over.
- *Mechanism:* `LEAD_IN` grows to 1.5 s when the setting or light changes. A heading option `hold=2`
  holds a reveal page silent, and is the default on the page after a `text=bottom` setup.
- *Check:* the storyboard's first panel of each new place shows at least 1.5 s before its first
  caption.

**12. Cause before reaction (S).**
- *Viewer:* nobody gasps at what hasn't happened yet.
- *Mechanism:* a review rule, `reaction-order`. A strong mood directed on a page before that page's
  only possible cause (an `enter=`, a shout, an `@sfx`, a faint) gets a `consider` telling the writer
  to move it below.
- *Check:* *The Visitor* page 2 is flagged.

**13. Crowding by drawn shape (S).**
- *Viewer:* separated silhouettes.
- *Mechanism:* `crowding` measures how much the composed puppets' drawn bounds overlap (the director
  already measures them) instead of x distance.
- *Check:* *The Visitor* page 5 is flagged, while x=0.5 and x=0.7 passes today.

**14. Page budget (S).**
- *Viewer:* no blank pages, and no empty "Meet the folk".
- *Mechanism:* `fables check` reports padding ("8 story pages leave 12 blank pages"). The meet page
  is left out when it would show nobody.
- *Check:* *The Visitor* proofs.

**15. Temperament per character (S).**
- *Viewer:* a fidgety kid and a slow grandad move differently in one style, which is Olrik's law of
  contrast made visible.
- *Mechanism:* a `[mo.motion]` table in `cast.toml` overrides a whitelisted set of `[motion]` dials
  (`idle-tempo`, `takes`, `overlap`, `travel` speed, `blink-every`).
- *Check:* `fables rig` acting test with two characters shows different timing.

**16. Story-flow recipes (M).**
- *Writer:* pick a shape and get a laid-out book.
- *Mechanism:* built-in `recipes/<flow>.md`, compiled in like styles, each with a `[flow]` table of
  beats with page and word budgets. `fables new "<Title>" --flow pile-up`. A world's `recipe.md` stays
  the default. The flows are in section 4.
- *Check:* each recipe's example proofs with no blank padding and no review fixes.

**17. Flow-aware review rules (M).**
- *Writer:* the review checks the shape the book claims to have.
- *Mechanism:* `refrain` (a line repeated three or more times, changed once), `goal-early` (the star
  speaks or reaches by page 2), `return-image` (the last setting equals the first, for circular and
  journey books), `wind-down` (bedtime: words, mood energy and light all fall over the last third, and
  no new actor appears in the last three pages). Each runs only when `[flow]` names its shape.
- *Check:* mutated copies of each example fail.

**18. Comic beats: `@hold` and `double-take` (M).**
- *Viewer:* gags get the pause before the payoff ([surprise gag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticipation_(animation))).
- *Mechanism:* `@hold 0.8` is a dead stop: no breath, no business, no music, unlike `@pause`, which only
  silences the voice. The `double-take` action glances, looks away, then snaps back into a take after
  about 0.3 s.
- *Check:* both appear in the rig acting test, and the gag-short recipe uses them.

**19. Turn towards each other (M).**
- *Viewer:* conversations look like conversations.
- *Mechanism:* in a two-way exchange with no `face=`, the acting layer turns speaker and listener a
  quarter-turn towards each other, and the stills use that facing.
- *Check:* on proof page 8, Mo and Hana are three-quarter towards each other.

**20. Hold the line (S).**
- *Viewer:* screen direction stays readable.
- *Mechanism:* a `screen-direction` review rule. Two figures who swap left and right between pages in
  one setting, without a walk, get a `consider`. The journey flow wants travel rightward out and
  leftward home.
- *Check:* a unit test with a swapped pair.

**21. Ambience from settings and light (M).**
- *Listener:* wind on the hill, rain in the storm, crickets at night.
- *Mechanism:* the setting root gets `data-ambience="birds,breeze"`, and light presets add rain or
  crickets. Beds are procedural noise like the existing `whoosh`, about 25 dB under narration
  ([bed levels](https://musicvivo.com/blog/music-under-voiceover), *practitioner*).
  `@scene ambience=none` turns them off.
- *Check:* listen to the storm page, and measure levels.

**22. Animatic in seconds (M).**
- *Writer and agent:* judge timing without rendering a video.
- *Mechanism:* `fables animatic <story>` holds the storyboard panels for their timeline durations
  over the offline narrator, music and effects, with burned-in labels. MCP `animatic_render`.
  Storyboard labels say `· push` and show the transition only on a page's first panel.
- *Check:* *The Visitor* animatic in under 10 s, with the same durations as the video.

**23. A style is a whole look (M).**
- *Viewer:* type, card, skin and transitions match the drawing.
- *Mechanism:* a `[page]` table in `style.toml`: `skin`, `card` (`paper`, `caption` or `none`),
  `typeface`, `transitions`, `effects`. A world inherits it unless it overrides.
- *Check:* Otto's page renders riso with a style-matched card and no `--style`.

**24. Three styles fables cannot draw yet (M each).**
- `silhouette` (shadow theatre): every figure an ink fill with visible pin joints, against `dusk` and
  `moonlit` gradient skies. Motion on twos with no squash. The cut-out rig becomes the look.
- `paper-theatre`: each part a card with a white cut edge and a hard drop shadow, with the far, near
  and front layers as visible card planes.
- `soft-flat`: no line, rounded shapes, one soft shade.
- *Check:* `fables style check` and `style sheet` for each.

## 4. Story flows to ship as recipes

Page counts target a 24-page print (about 18 story pages after front and back matter). Words are per
page for ages 3–5. Example premises are original.

### Three Tries (problem and solution)
| Beat | Pages | Words | Stage and camera |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Home and the want, said aloud | 2 | 25–35 | wide, then medium on the star `talk`; `day` |
| Try 1 fails | 2 | 25–35 | same setting and staging every try, so the variation reads; close reaction |
| Try 2 fails bigger | 2 | 25–35 | same staging; `@hold` before the failure |
| Try 3 fails biggest: the low point | 2 | 20–30 | darkest light (`overcast`/`storm`); star `sad droop`, full shot alone |
| Help or insight (a friend, a small thing noticed) | 2 | 25–35 | `notice`, then `think`; `dawn` |
| Try 4 succeeds, using what failed | 3 | 25–35 | push in on the attempt; pull out wide on success |
| New normal and button (the refrain, changed) | 2 | 15–25 | the opening staging, star now `proud`; `golden` |

*Premise:* Pip the snail wants to see the fireworks over the garden wall. Climbing fails (slides),
riding the cat fails (naps), and the tallest sunflower fails (bends). In the dark she notices how the
flowers turn towards light. On the fourth try every snail climbs the sunflower together, and their
weight bends it right over the wall, just in time.

### The Pile-Up (cumulative)
| Beat | Pages | Words | Stage and camera |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The quiet start, one actor | 1 | 15–20 | wide, one figure left of centre facing right |
| First link | 1 | 15–25 | the newcomer `enter=right`; locked wide |
| Links 2 to 6, each repeating the whole chain | 6 | 25 → 45 | one `enter=right` a page; the line grows left to right; a slow push tightens a little each page |
| The topple | 1 | 3–8 | `**SHOUT**`, `impact=1`, `shake=0.6`, `@sfx bonk`, music stops |
| Aftermath, everyone in a heap | 1 | 10–20 | full shot, a `@hold` of silence, then `laugh` |
| Button that starts the chain again | 1 | 10–15 | the opening framing, with one new arrival at the edge |

*Premise:* In Mumbleton the wind steals words but not tunes, so Mo hums. The kettle hums back, then
the cat purrs along, the bus brakes squeal in tune, the clock bongs the beat and the goose honks the
high note, until the whole street is one song so loud the bunting snaps off its string. In the hush
afterwards, a tiny bird hums Mo's first note back to him.

### There and Back (journey and return)
| Beat | Pages | Words | Stage and camera |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Home and the reason to go | 2 | 25–35 | home setting, `day` |
| Setting out | 1 | 15–25 | `exit=right`; all outward travel moves rightward |

*Premise:* A letter arrives at the Hoppington post office with no address, only a drawing of a door.
Clem hops it to the door on the mill, the door in the hill and the door in the lighthouse, and none

## 5. What to leave alone

- **More cutting and more camera moves.** The research rewards calm. The 1.2 s floor and the
  one-move-a-scene advice are right ([pacing review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11044375/)).
  Children of every age tested struggle with narrative discontinuity ([film cuts, ages 4–8](https://doi.org/10.1027/1016-9040/a000098)).
- **More moods, mouth shapes or frames.** Fifteen moods and the nine-shape chart are the industry
  kit. What is missing is timing between moods, not more of them. Limited animation is a choice, not a
  lack ([limited animation](https://animationobsessive.substack.com/p/dont-call-it-limited-animation)).
- **True camera angles and perspective.** A cut-out stage cannot tilt honestly, and faking high and
  low angles costs a lot for little.
- **Two-page spreads.** The readme names them as a limit. Square single pages work for ebooks and
  video, so revisit them only if print demands it.
- **Generated backgrounds or image-model art.** That breaks the on-model, offline and repeatable
  promise that makes fables what it is.
- **Fourth-wall questions on every page.** Participation cues help, but their effect is debated
  ([participatory cues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Participatory_cues)). One `@ask` moment a book is
  plenty.
- **Songs as the teaching tool.** Songs help word-for-word recall after repeats, but spoken prose
  helps understanding ([Calvert 2001](https://cdmc.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/Calvert-2001.pdf)).
  Keep the narrator for meaning, and use music for mood.
- **Cloth, physics and lighting simulation.** Follow-through and the light presets already carry it.
